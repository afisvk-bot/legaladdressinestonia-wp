## content_blocks_0_content
Registering a company in Estonia does not protect your brand name. A business name in the Commercial Register and a trademark are different rights: the first only stops another Estonian company from taking the same name, while the second gives an exclusive right to the sign for specific goods and services. Without a trademark, you can lose your brand in a dispute with a marketplace, a competitor or a copycat.

Filing the application itself takes an hour. Everything is decided by the questions before it: which jurisdiction to choose (Estonia, the whole EU or an international registration); which classes to list so that the mark protects your real business and not just a wording; whether similar marks already exist that EUIPO will not tell you about. A mistake here surfaces several months later as an opposition or a refusal, when the fee has already been paid.
<blockquote>We check the sign, choose the jurisdiction and classes, prepare and file the application with the Estonian Patent Office (Patendiamet) or EUIPO, follow it through to registration and keep track of renewal deadlines. We handle patents, utility models and disputes together with licensed patent attorneys.</blockquote>

## content_blocks_8_title_right
Key insight: you register not a word, but a list of goods and services
## content_blocks_8_text_right
Owners think of a trademark as a name. The office and the courts think of it as a list of classes and wording, and that is what defines what you have protected.
<ul>
<li><b>Too narrow a list.</b> The mark is registered for "software", but the business sells a subscription to an online service. A competitor with a similar name in a neighbouring class formally does not infringe your right.</li>
<li><b>Too broad a list.</b> Classes added "just in case" cost money and increase the risk of oppositions, and after five years of non-use the mark can be challenged for those goods and services.</li>
<li><b>No search before filing.</b> EUIPO does not check conflicts with earlier marks itself: their owners do that through oppositions. An opposition arrives after publication, when the fee has already been paid.</li>
<li><b>The mark in a private person's name instead of the company's.</b> For marketplaces, investors and a sale of the business, ownership matters. Transferring the mark later is a separate procedure with its own costs.</li>
</ul>
<b>Practical conclusion:</b> the decisive part of the work happens before filing: search, classes, wording, owner. We start with that; the filing itself is then a technical step.
## content_blocks_9_title_left
When trademark registration is not needed and when it is not enough
## content_blocks_9_text_left
An honest review saves more than the fee.
<ul>
<li><b>A descriptive name.</b> "Best Accountant" or "Fast Delivery" lack distinctiveness: the office will refuse and the fee will not be refunded. It is better to spend the time on the name.</li>
<li><b>Estonia only, but sales across the EU.</b> An Estonian mark does not protect you in Germany or France. EU marketplaces usually require an EU trademark.</li>
<li><b>EU trademark only, but sales in the UK and the US.</b> Since Brexit, an EU trademark does not apply in the UK; the US is a separate jurisdiction.</li>
<li><b>A technical solution.</b> A trademark protects a sign, not an invention. A device or method needs a patent or a utility model, and the appearance of a product needs an industrial design.</li>
<li><b>The name is already in use.</b> If a similar mark is registered for the same goods, your registration will almost certainly be challenged. Sometimes it is cheaper to change the name before launch than to fight a dispute.</li>
</ul>

<h2><b>How to register a trademark in Estonia and the EU: practice</b></h2>
<h3><b>Process in Estonia</b></h3>
<ol>
<li><b>Search and preparation.</b> Checking earlier marks and business names, choosing classes and wording.</li>
<li><b>Filing with Patendiamet.</b> Online; the fee is €145 per class and €45 for each additional class. Word, figurative and 3D marks can use the fast-track e-filing procedure.</li>
<li><b>Examination.</b> The office examines absolute grounds: distinctiveness and descriptiveness. It no longer searches for conflicts with earlier marks on its own.</li>
<li><b>Publication.</b> The registration decision is published in Eesti Kaubamärgileht.</li>
<li><b>Opposition.</b> Within 2 months, owners of earlier rights may file an opposition.</li>
<li><b>Registration.</b> The certificate is issued electronically; the mark is valid for 10 years and can be renewed.</li>
</ol>
The language of proceedings is Estonian; the application can be filed in another language with a translation submitted within two months. A person with no residence, seat or establishment in Estonia acts through an Estonian patent attorney. An Estonian OÜ of a foreign owner is a seat in Estonia, so the company can file the application itself.

Renewal is every 10 years; the renewal fee is the same €850 for the first class. Renewal is possible in the last six months of the term or in a six-month grace period after it, with a surcharge.
<h3><b>International registration</b></h3>
An Estonian company can file an international application under the Madrid System based on an Estonian application or registration, online, designating the countries it needs. Fees are paid to WIPO and the national offices. In the first years, the international registration depends on the basic one: if the basic mark falls away, the international registration suffers too.
<h3><b>Patents, utility models and designs</b></h3>
A patent protects an invention for up to 20 years and goes through full examination, which is expensive and slow. A utility model in Estonia is registered faster and more cheaply, without substantive examination, and protects a technical solution for a shorter period. An industrial design protects the appearance of a product, packaging or interface, in Estonia or across the whole EU at once. These procedures require a patent attorney; we coordinate the work with our partners and link the rights to the corporate structure.
<h3><b>Trademarks and marketplaces</b></h3>
Major marketplaces give access to brand protection programmes only to owners of a registered or filed trademark. For sellers on European platforms, an EU trademark is usually a first step rather than a formality.

## content_blocks_12_faq_items_3_question

## content_blocks_12_faq_items_3_answer
No. A business name in the Commercial Register and a trademark are different rights. Only trademark registration gives an exclusive right to the sign for goods and services.

## content_blocks_12_faq_items_4_answer
If you have no residence or seat in Estonia, yes. An Estonian company can file an application with Patendiamet itself.
## content_blocks_12_faq_items_5_question
Which should I choose: an Estonian trademark or an EU trademark?
## content_blocks_12_faq_items_5_answer
If you sell only in Estonia, an Estonian mark is cheaper. If you sell across the EU or on marketplaces, usually an EU trademark.
## content_blocks_12_faq_items_6_question
Can I register a mark if a similar one already exists?
## content_blocks_12_faq_items_6_answer
It depends on how similar the signs and the goods are. If a similar mark is registered for the same goods, the risk of opposition is high, so the search must be done before filing.
## content_blocks_12_faq_items_7_question
Who should own the mark: me or the company?
## content_blocks_12_faq_items_7_answer
For a business, usually the company: it makes things easier with marketplaces, investors and a sale of the business. Sometimes the mark is held in a holding company and licensed to the operating company.
## content_blocks_12_faq_items_8_question
How long is a trademark valid?
## content_blocks_12_faq_items_8_answer
10 years from the filing date, then renewable for further 10-year periods with no limit on the number of renewals.
