## content_blocks_0_content
You cannot simply "abandon" an Estonian company. As long as it is entered in the register, it has to file an annual report, keep a legal address and a contact person and, if it is VAT-registered, submit a KMD every month. A company that is no longer serviced does not disappear: it gets fines of up to €3,200, which also fall on management board members personally, and then compulsory deletion on the register's terms rather than yours.

There is always a proper way out, and more than one. The company can be liquidated, which takes at least six to seven months by law. An inactive company with no history can sometimes be deleted more simply. The company can be sold to a new owner. And a company that has already been deleted can be restored if money, assets or contracts remain in it.
<blockquote>We choose the route that fits the company's actual situation, put the accounting and taxes in order, carry out the liquidation from the shareholders' resolution to deletion from the register, and watch every statutory deadline.</blockquote>

## content_blocks_8_title_right
Key insight: liquidations are broken by taxes, not by the register
## content_blocks_8_text_right
The legal side of liquidation is a sequence of deadlines that can be planned. Mistakes happen in the accounting side, which runs in parallel throughout these months.
<ul>
<li><b>The books do not stop.</b> The liquidation resolution closes the financial year and opens a new one. So you will need at least one more set of reports, plus a KMD every month if the company has not been deregistered from VAT.</li>
<li><b>Tax on payouts to shareholders.</b> A liquidation payout is taxed at 22/78, but only on the part exceeding contributed capital. If capital contributions are not documented, more may be taxed than necessary.</li>
<li><b>Loose ends at EMTA cannot be fixed after deletion.</b> Once the company is deleted, the tax authority can no longer process a refund, a correction or a late return without a separate restoration procedure. All tax returns are closed before the deletion application.</li>
<li><b>Bank accounts and subscriptions.</b> A balance on an account that has not been closed is company property. Discovering it after deletion is a classic reason for a repeat liquidation.</li>
</ul>
<b>Practical conclusion:</b> plan the liquidation backwards from the tax end: which returns, which payout, which tax. We start with that, and the register follows the schedule.
## content_blocks_9_title_left
When liquidation is not needed and when it is not enough
## content_blocks_9_text_left
An honest review saves more than the procedure itself.
<ul>
<li><b>The company never started operating.</b> A full liquidation with a creditor period may be excessive: there is a separate deletion procedure for such OÜs.</li>
<li><b>The company has value.</b> If there is a buyer, contracts, a domain, a history or a licence, a sale is often faster and more profitable than liquidation. As a rule, a transfer of an OÜ share is executed before a notary.</li>
<li><b>The company may be needed again.</b> You can keep it, but the obligations do not "sleep": the annual report, address and contact person remain. If there is no turnover, it makes sense to deregister the company from VAT.</li>
<li><b>Debts exceed assets.</b> This is bankruptcy, not liquidation. Liquidators must file a bankruptcy petition if the assets are insufficient to satisfy creditors' claims.</li>
<li><b>Reports for past years have not been filed.</b> A company with open periods cannot be liquidated: first <a href="{L:4578}">accounting restoration</a>, then liquidation. We handle this as one project.</li>
</ul>

The deadlines overlap, so the fastest lawful route takes about six to seven months. A realistic plan is 6–12 months, allowing for the sale of assets, final tax returns and EMTA questions.
<h3><b>Taxes on liquidation</b></h3>
A payout to shareholders on liquidation is subject to 22/78 income tax at company level, on the part exceeding monetary and non-monetary contributions to capital. Contributed capital is returned tax-free. The reduced 14/86 rate no longer exists since 2025. The final TSD for payments to employees, VAT deregistration and the last KMD are closed separately.
<h3><b>Deletion of an inactive company</b></h3>
For an OÜ that never started operating, there is a separate, simpler route of deletion from the register, without a liquidation period or liquidation reports. The condition is the absence of activity, assets and obligations. We first check that this is really the case: a single subscription or bank fee is already a transaction.
<h3><b>Selling the company instead of liquidating it</b></h3>
As a rule, a share in an OÜ is transferred by a notarised contract. The exception: if the share capital is at least €10,000, fully paid up, and the articles of association, by unanimous decision of the shareholders, exclude the notarial form, the transfer is made through the register. The buyer carries out a due diligence of the company, so the accounting and reports must be in order before a sale.
<h3><b>What happens if you do nothing</b></h3>
The register can fine the company and each board member personally, up to €3,200 per violation, repeatedly. Then compulsory deletion begins. Fines already imposed do not disappear with the company. If after deletion it turns out that the company had assets, a supplementary liquidation is carried out, this time through a court and with a liquidator appointed by it.
<h3><b>Restoring a deleted company</b></h3>
A deleted company can be restored if money, assets or rights remain in it, or if it needs to be brought back into operation. The state fee is €200, but the main work is removing the reason for deletion: filing reports, appointing a contact person, settling taxes.

## content_blocks_12_faq_items_0_answer
By law, at least about six to seven months from the shareholders' resolution to the deletion application. In practice, 6–12 months, including final tax returns and the sale of assets.

Can a company be closed faster?
## content_blocks_12_faq_items_1_answer
If the OÜ never started operating and has no obligations, it can be deleted under a simplified procedure. If there is a buyer, selling the company is faster than liquidating it.
## content_blocks_12_faq_items_2_question

## content_blocks_12_faq_items_2_answer
Yes, but only the amount exceeding contributed capital: the company pays 22/78. Contributed capital is returned tax-free.

## content_blocks_12_faq_items_3_answer
By default, the management board members. The shareholders or a court may appoint another person. If the board members are abroad or do not want to run the procedure, our specialist can act as liquidator.

## content_blocks_12_faq_items_4_answer

## content_blocks_12_faq_items_5_question

## content_blocks_12_faq_items_5_answer
The register will fine the company and the board members personally, up to €3,200 per violation and repeatedly, and then start compulsory deletion. The fines are not cancelled.
## content_blocks_12_faq_items_6_question
Can a deleted company be restored?
## content_blocks_12_faq_items_6_answer
Yes. The state fee is €200, but the reason for deletion must be removed: file the missing reports, appoint a contact person, settle taxes.

Liquidation is not suitable in this case: a bankruptcy petition is required. We will assess the situation and bring in specialised lawyers.

## content_blocks_12_faq_items_8_answer
As a rule, yes. The exception is share capital of at least €10,000, fully paid up, and a clause in the articles waiving the notarial form, adopted unanimously.
