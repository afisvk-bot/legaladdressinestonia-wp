## content_blocks_0_content
Changing the owner of an Estonian company is not as simple as setting it up. As a general rule, an agreement to transfer a share in an OÜ is concluded in notarial form, and the other shareholders have a pre-emptive right to buy the share before an outside buyer. A mistake in the form of the deal makes it void, and a mistake in the procedure opens the door to a dispute.

There are, however, several routes. The deal can be signed at a notary in Estonia or remotely via e-Notary with an e-resident card. If the share capital is at least €10,000, fully paid and the articles allow it, the share can be transferred without a notary at all, through the register. And sometimes it is more convenient to change the shareholders through a capital reduction combined with a simultaneous increase.
<blockquote>We choose the route for your deal, check the company before the sale, prepare the agreement and documents, support the signing and update everything that changes afterwards: the shareholder register, beneficial owner data, the board and the bank.</blockquote>

## content_blocks_7_title_right
Key insight: it is not the notary that costs you, but what was not checked before the deal
## content_blocks_7_text_right
Buyers of a share in an existing company often check only the price. Sellers check only the form of the agreement. Then it turns out:
<ul>
<li><b>The pre-emptive right.</b> Unless the articles exclude it, other shareholders can buy the share on the same terms. A deal that bypasses this right is grounds for a dispute.</li>
<li><b>Unfiled reports and tax debts.</b> The new owner gets the company with its whole history: register penalties, EMTA debts, unclosed periods.</li>
<li><b>Unpaid shares.</b> If the capital contribution has not been made, the obligation to pay it may pass to the buyer.</li>
<li><b>The board and the bank.</b> The share has been transferred, but the former owner remains a board member with access to the account.</li>
<li><b>The seller's tax.</b> In Estonia the deal itself creates no company-level tax, but the seller pays capital gains tax in their country. For residents of Ukraine, there is also a CFC notification within 60 days.</li>
</ul>
<b>Practical takeaway:</b> before the deal, the articles, the register, the tax position and the bank are checked. We do this as part of the package, not after the problems have become the buyer's property.

<h2><b>How to sell a share in an Estonian OÜ: in practice</b></h2>
<h3><b>Step-by-step process</b></h3>
<ol>
<li><b>Review of the articles and the register.</b> Form of the deal, pre-emptive right, transfer restrictions, whether the shares are paid up.</li>
<li><b>Company check.</b> Annual reports, tax position, contracts, the bank.</li>
<li><b>Notice to shareholders.</b> If the pre-emptive right applies, an offer to the shareholders or their written waiver.</li>

<li><b>After the deal.</b> UBO data within 30 days, the bank, e-MTA, the accounting.</li>
</ol>

<h3><b>e-Notary</b></h3>
Estonian notaries conduct deals remotely: the parties join by video call and sign the documents with an e-resident card or an Estonian ID card. This is the standard route for owners abroad when a simplified transfer is not possible.
<h3><b>Taxes on the sale of a share</b></h3>
The share transfer itself creates no tax at company level. A seller who is an individual pays capital gains tax in their country of tax residence. Estonia taxes a non-resident's gain mainly when the company's value consists largely of Estonian real estate. An Estonian resident declares the gain and pays 22%. Read more in our article on <a href="{L:1037}">taxes for companies in Estonia</a>.
<h3><b>Obligations after the deal</b></h3>
The board must update the beneficial owner data within 30 days. Residents of Ukraine who acquired or lost a share file a CFC notification within 60 days; see the page <a href="{L:4757}">for Ukrainian entrepreneurs</a>. Banks and payment services must be notified of the change of owner, otherwise the account may be blocked at a routine review.
## content_blocks_11_faq_items_0_question
Do I need a notary to sell a share in an OÜ?
## content_blocks_11_faq_items_0_answer
As a general rule, yes. The exception is capital of €10,000 or more, fully paid, with a provision in the articles excluding the notarial form, adopted unanimously.
## content_blocks_11_faq_items_1_question
Can I sell a share without coming to Estonia?
## content_blocks_11_faq_items_1_answer
Yes. e-residents sign the deal via e-Notary by video call; without e-Residency, by a power of attorney notarised abroad.
## content_blocks_11_faq_items_2_question
What is the shareholders' pre-emptive right?
## content_blocks_11_faq_items_2_answer
The right of other shareholders to buy the share before an outside buyer on the same terms, unless the articles provide otherwise.

Via the e-Business Register, usually one business day. At a notary, the time to book and prepare, plus 1–3 days for the register changes.
