## content_blocks_0_content
Since 2023 an Estonian OÜ can be set up with capital from €0.01, and most e-resident companies did exactly that. But sooner or later the capital has to be increased: to let in an investor, get a loan or a licence, transfer shares without a notary, or simply show the bank and partners that the company stands on its own feet.

A capital increase is one of the most formal changes in the register. A shareholder resolution, new articles, payment of the contribution specifically to the company's account, an application within six months, and a valuation for an in-kind contribution. The registrar refuses the entry even when the money arrived in the wrong account. And on the tax side there is a detail that is rarely mentioned: the contribution has to be declared in the TSD, otherwise it cannot later be returned to the shareholders tax-free.
<blockquote>We choose the method of increase for your goal, prepare the resolution and the articles, monitor the payment and the filing, and after the entry record the contribution in the accounts and declare it to EMTA.</blockquote>

## content_blocks_7_title_right
Key insight: the contribution must be not only paid but also declared
## content_blocks_7_text_right
One detail separates a correctly increased capital from a merely increased one.
<ul>
<li><b>Money only to the company's account.</b> The register will not enter the increase if the cash contribution did not arrive in the company's own account. A court confirmed such a refusal in 2025.</li>
<li><b>Six months.</b> The application is filed no later than six months after the resolution. Miss it, and the resolution has to be adopted again.</li>
<li><b>TSD, annex 7.</b> Capital contributions are reported in annex 7 of the TSD return. It is this record that later allows the invested amount to be returned to shareholders tax-free, on a capital reduction or liquidation. If the contribution was not declared, the tax authority may treat the return as a profit distribution taxed at 22/78.</li>
<li><b>In-kind contribution and valuation.</b> Overvaluing the asset creates liability for the shareholder and the board; undervaluing it deprives the company of capital on its balance sheet.</li>
</ul>
<b>Practical takeaway:</b> a capital increase is both the register and the tax records. We handle both parts as one project.

<h2><b>How to increase the capital of an Estonian OÜ: in practice</b></h2>
<h3><b>Step-by-step process</b></h3>
<ol>
<li><b>Review of the articles.</b> Whether they state an exact capital amount or a permitted range, and whether there is a pre-emptive right.</li>
<li><b>Shareholder resolution.</b> The amount of the increase, who contributes and how much, the nominal value of new shares, the premium.</li>
<li><b>New articles.</b> If the capital amount goes beyond the limits stated in the articles.</li>
<li><b>Payment.</b> A cash contribution to the company's account; in kind, transfer of the asset and valuation.</li>
<li><b>Application to the register.</b> Filed by a board member no later than 6 months after the resolution, with proof of payment.</li>
<li><b>Entry.</b> Usually 1–3 business days; state fee of about €25.</li>
<li><b>Accounts and taxes.</b> The contribution is recorded in the books and in annex 7 of the TSD; if control changes, UBO data is updated.</li>
</ol>
<h3><b>Cash contribution</b></h3>
The money is transferred to a bank or payment institution account in the company's name. Proof of payment is attached to the application. The registrar will not accept a transfer to a board member's personal account or to another company's account.
<h3><b>In-kind contribution</b></h3>
A contribution can be any asset that can be valued in money: equipment, intellectual property rights, shares in another company, a monetary claim against the company such as a shareholder loan. The value is determined by a valuation; in some cases an auditor reviews it. Work and services cannot be contributed to capital.
<h3><b>Bonus issue</b></h3>
The capital is increased from retained earnings or reserves, with no new money. This changes the structure of equity but not the tax history: amounts transferred from profit do not become shareholder contributions for the purpose of a tax-free return.
<h3><b>Entry of a new shareholder</b></h3>
The investor receives new shares in exchange for a contribution to the company. If the articles do not exclude the pre-emptive right, existing shareholders must waive or exercise it. The investor's rights (share classes, governance, anti-dilution protection) are set out in the articles and a shareholders' agreement.
<h3><b>Taxes</b></h3>
A capital contribution is not taxed for either the company or the shareholder. It is important to declare it in annex 7 of the TSD: the record of contributions determines how much can be returned to shareholders without the 22/78 tax on a capital reduction or liquidation. Read more in our article on <a href="{L:844}">corporate tax in Estonia in 2026</a>.

## content_blocks_11_faq_items_1_answer
The state fee for changing an OÜ's data is usually €25. Add the cost of preparing documents and, for an in-kind contribution, the valuation.
## content_blocks_11_faq_items_2_question

## content_blocks_11_faq_items_2_answer
Yes: equipment, IP, shares in another company or a claim against the company. The contribution is valued, in some cases with an auditor's review.
## content_blocks_11_faq_items_3_question

## content_blocks_11_faq_items_3_answer
Yes, the loan claim can be an in-kind contribution.
## content_blocks_11_faq_items_4_question

## content_blocks_11_faq_items_4_answer
No. But it must be declared in annex 7 of the TSD so that it can later be returned tax-free.

## content_blocks_11_faq_items_8_answer
