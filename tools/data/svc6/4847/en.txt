## content_blocks_0_content
Since 2023 the minimum capital of an Estonian OÜ has been €0.01. Companies that once paid in €2,500 or more can now return that money to the shareholders, and with correct records do so tax-free. A capital reduction is also used for a shareholder's exit, to cover losses and as part of a change of owners without a notary.

But it is the only capital change that affects creditors' interests. That is why the law requires them to be notified, gives them time to file claims, and only then allows the entry to be made and the money paid out. A mistake in the procedure makes the payout unlawful, and a mistake in the records turns the return of a contribution into a profit distribution taxed at 22/78.
<blockquote>We check how much capital can be returned tax-free, prepare the resolution and articles, publish the creditor notice, file with the register at the right time and declare the payout in the TSD.</blockquote>

## content_blocks_11_content
<h2><b>How to reduce the capital of an Estonian OÜ: in practice</b></h2>
<h3><b>Step-by-step process</b></h3>
<ol>
<li><b>Company check.</b> The articles, declared contributions, net assets, creditors.</li>
<li><b>Shareholder resolution.</b> The new capital amount, the method of reduction, the payout to each shareholder.</li>
<li><b>New articles.</b> If the capital amount in the articles changes.</li>
<li><b>Creditor notice.</b> Publication in Ametlikud Teadaanded and letters to known creditors inviting them to file claims.</li>
<li><b>Application to the register.</b> After the statutory period from the resolution has expired.</li>
<li><b>Register entry.</b> State fee usually about €25.</li>
<li><b>Payout and reporting.</b> After the entry and with creditors' claims taken into account; reported in annex 7 of the TSD.</li>
</ol>
<h3><b>Methods of reduction</b></h3>
The capital can be reduced by lowering the nominal value of all shares or by cancelling individual shares, for example the share of an exiting shareholder. The nominal value of a share cannot fall below the set minimum, and the capital cannot fall below €0.01.
<h3><b>Creditor protection</b></h3>
A capital reduction lowers the amount that secures the company's obligations. That is why the law gives creditors a period to file claims and prohibits payouts to shareholders before the register entry. A board member who allows a payout in breach of these rules is liable to the company and its creditors.
<h3><b>Taxes</b></h3>
A payout to shareholders on a capital reduction is subject to 22/78 tax only to the extent it exceeds the declared contributions to equity. For example, if a shareholder contributed €19,000 and received €16,600 on the reduction, no tax arises, and the remaining contribution balance carries forward. The records are kept in annex 7 of the TSD. A reduction without payouts is not reported in annex 7.
<h3><b>Reduction and increase at the same time</b></h3>
When shareholders need to change, the capital is under €10,000 and a notary is undesirable, the exiting shareholder's share can be cancelled through a capital reduction and the new shareholder admitted through an increase. These are two procedures with their own timelines that are planned together.

## content_blocks_7_title_right
Key insight: what is returned tax-free is not the capital but the declared contributions
## content_blocks_7_text_right
Owners often assume: "I paid in €2,500, so €2,500 will come back tax-free." That is true only on one condition.
<ul>
<li><b>Contribution records in the TSD.</b> The tax authority looks not at the capital amount in the register but at the contributions reported in annex 7 of the TSD. If the contribution on incorporation or a capital increase was not declared, the payout may be treated as a profit distribution.</li>
<li><b>The amount above contributions.</b> If more is paid out than was contributed, the difference is taxed at 22/78 as a profit distribution.</li>
<li><b>A company set up without paying in capital.</b> If the capital was never paid in, there is nothing to return, and the payout will be profit subject to tax.</li>
<li><b>The order of deadlines.</b> A payout before the register entry or without regard to creditors' claims is unlawful, and the board member is personally liable for it.</li>
</ul>
<b>Practical takeaway:</b> before adopting the resolution, check the contribution history in the TSD. We do this as the first step and give you the exact tax-free amount.
## content_blocks_8_title_left
When a capital reduction is not needed and when it is not enough
## content_blocks_8_text_left
<ul>
<li><b>You want to take out profit, not capital.</b> For that there are dividends based on an approved annual report, with 22/78 tax in any case. See <a href="{L:844}">corporate tax in Estonia in 2026</a>.</li>
<li><b>The company is closing.</b> If the business has ended, contributions are returned as part of the <a href="{L:4758}">liquidation</a>, and a separate capital reduction is not needed.</li>
<li><b>Net assets are below half of the capital.</b> A capital reduction is one of the measures the shareholders must consider, but payouts are not possible in that situation.</li>
<li><b>A shareholder sells their share to someone else.</b> That is a <a href="{L:4845}">share transfer</a>: the seller receives money from the buyer, and the company pays nothing.</li>
<li><b>Creditors have filed claims.</b> If creditors have filed claims, they must be satisfied or secured before any payout to shareholders.</li>
</ul>
